## Puff Count — the one that got here first

If you have an iPhone and you have searched this category, you have already met it. Puff
Count has been on the App Store since 2019, it holds **4.3 stars across 2,600 ratings**, and
it does the core job properly: a tap per puff, nicotine in mg from your device strength,
daily and weekly graphs, a daily limit, and a quit plan built around a date you choose. It
also has quit buddies, which is a genuinely good idea and something Cirrus deliberately
does not have.

It is iPhone only. There is no Android version.

Two things are worth knowing before you subscribe. The first is that its App Store privacy
card lists Usage Data under **"Data Used to Track You"** — meaning across apps and websites
owned by other companies. That is ordinary for a free app and it is disclosed properly; it
is simply a different trade than the one some people think they are making.

The second is that the listing claims over 800,000 users in the description and over 375,000
in the release notes, on the same page, on the same day. Neither figure carries a source.
This is not a Puff Count problem so much as a category problem — uncited numbers are the
norm here, and it is the single reason we have a rule against them.

**Best for:** an iPhone user who wants a proven per-puff counter and a friend to compete with.

## Kwit — the one that is not really a puff counter

Kwit is the biggest name of the three by a distance: **1M+ installs and 4.6 stars from 19,300
reviews** on Google Play, on both platforms, ad-free, built on cognitive behavioural therapy
and heavily gamified. It covers vaping explicitly — it tracks e-cigarette dosage and nicotine
substitutes alongside cigarettes.

What it does not do is count puffs. Kwit logs cravings, consumption entries and dosage. If
you are quitting cigarettes, that model is the right one, and Kwit executes it better than
anything else at this size. If you are quitting a disposable, "cigarettes not smoked" is
measuring the wrong unit — a disposable is not a pack of 20, and the number a cigarette app
shows you is a conversion, not a count.

Its pricing is the widest of the three and the hardest to summarise, which we come back to
below.

**Best for:** someone quitting cigarettes, or someone who wants badges, streaks and a
structured CBT programme more than a puff number.

## Cirrus — ours, and what it does differently

Cirrus counts puffs the way Puff Count does, and then does three things with them that the
other two do not.

**The plan bends instead of resetting.** Every quit app has to answer one question: what
happens on a bad day? Cirrus builds a daily puff limit from a baseline you counted rather
than a goal you guessed, then keeps recalculating it. Beat your limits and it speeds up.
Struggle two days running and it adds runway and moves your Freedom Day rather than setting
you up to fail. Streaks earn a repair token every seven clean days, and a bad day spends a
token instead of erasing three weeks.

That is not a small preference. Losing a twelve-day streak to one rough night is how quit
apps make people delete quit apps.

**You can log without opening it.** An Android home screen widget logs a puff in one tap,
with a five-second undo for the mistaps, and you can edit any past day afterwards. Both are
things Puff Count reviewers have asked for repeatedly on the App Store — one of the most
helpful reviews on the listing is a request for exactly this. The iOS widget and an Apple
Watch app are built and ship with the iOS version.

**The free tier is documented, not implied.** The counter, the widget, the daily limit, the
streak, money saved, the recovery timeline and the community are free permanently. Premium
buys the unlimited AI coach and Panic Button, the fully adaptive plan, craving forecasts and
unlimited history.

And the honest side of the ledger: Cirrus is **Android only today**, it launched this month,
and it has no reviews. Puff Count has six years of iPhone users behind it and Kwit has a
million installs. If you are on an iPhone right now, two of these three apps exist for you
and Cirrus is not one of them.

**Best for:** an Android vaper who wants a real per-puff count and a plan that survives a bad
night.

## How to actually choose

The feature grids in this category all say roughly the same thing, so use the questions that
separate them instead. We wrote those up separately in
[how to choose a puff counter app](/blog/how-to-choose-a-puff-counter-app), and the short
form is four questions:

1. **Does it start from a number you counted, or one you guessed?** A goal set on day one
   measures your optimism.
2. **Does it cite anything?** Count the sourced statistics in an app's marketing. It is
   usually zero, and an app willing to invent its success rate has no claim on your trust
   for the numbers it shows about you.
3. **What happens on a bad day?** Ask this first, because it is the one you will live with.
4. **Is the free tier usable, or is it a trial?** Counting, your limit and your streak are
   the core. Behind a paywall, that is not a free tier.

And then the practical filter that decides it for most people anyway: which phone is in your
hand. Puff Count if it is an iPhone, Cirrus if it is Android, Kwit if you want the CBT
programme and do not need a per-puff number.

None of the three is a treatment. The strongest evidence in vaping cessation combines
medication, behavioural support and tracking; an app is the tracking layer and the layer that
gets you through the fifteen minutes a craving lasts. If you are considering nicotine
replacement or medication, that conversation belongs with a pharmacist or a doctor.
