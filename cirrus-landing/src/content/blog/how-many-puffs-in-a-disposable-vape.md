Modern disposables advertise anywhere from 600 puffs to well over 20,000. Those numbers are
printed in large friendly type on the front of the box, and they are the least useful
information on it.

## Why the box number is optimistic

Advertised counts come from a machine. It takes short, evenly spaced puffs of a fixed
duration, and it counts until the device stops producing aerosol.

You are not a machine. Real draws are longer, harder and less regular, and a longer draw
pulls more liquid across the coil. So a device rated at 6,000 puffs commonly gives a real
person something closer to 3,000 or 4,000. Nobody is lying to you exactly, but the test
conditions and your Tuesday have very little to do with each other.

Two other things shorten it: leaving the device somewhere hot, which thins the liquid, and
a coil that starts to burn before the tank is empty. Both are common and neither is on the
box.

## Puffs are the wrong unit

There's a more useful way to think about it. What matters isn't how many times you can press
the thing. It's how much nicotine is inside it.

> A Geek Bar Pulse holds about 800mg of nicotine. Adjusted for what your body absorbs,
> that's roughly 300 to 500 cigarettes in one device.

The Pulse X is around 900mg. You'll see "a thousand cigarettes" quoted online. That figure
divides raw puff counts and it overstates the case. Three to five hundred is the honest
number, and it's still a lot to be carrying around next to your keys.

The reason a device can hold that much at all is nicotine salts. Adding an organic acid
strips out the throat burn that used to stop people going above about 20mg/ml. That's the
entire innovation. Not flavour, not battery life.

## What's in there besides nicotine

Worth knowing, since you asked how much you're getting through: researchers at UC Davis
tested seven disposables and found one released more lead in a single day of use than
roughly 20 packs of cigarettes. Nickel exceeded cancer-risk thresholds in every device
tested.

We went through that study and four other categories of device in
[our ranking of the most dangerous vapes](/blog/most-dangerous-vapes-ranked-2026), including
the part where one brand had used a leaded bronze alloy for a component that isn't even the
coil.

There's also a legal wrinkle most people don't know about. **97.6% of disposable sales** in
tracked US stores are products the FDA has never authorized. Not banned, not recalled,
simply never reviewed. Among fruit, candy, sweet and mint flavours, not a single authorized
product exists.

## The number worth tracking

Device ratings tell you about the device. They tell you nothing about you.

For a figure that's any use, count your puffs across three ordinary days and divide the device
rating by your daily average. Now the box number means something,
because it's attached to your behaviour rather than a machine's.

Most people guess low when they try this, often by half. We wrote separately about
[what a heavy daily puff count actually looks like](/blog/how-many-puffs-a-day-is-a-lot) and
why the time of your first puff tells you more than the total.
