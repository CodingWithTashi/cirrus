Search this question and you'll get a confident number. Two hundred is moderate. Over three
hundred is heavy. Under a hundred is fine.

Check who's publishing them. Almost every one is a vape shop.

No health body publishes a safe or normal daily puff count, because there isn't one to
publish. Puff sizes aren't comparable between devices, nicotine strengths range from 3mg to
50mg per millilitre, and nobody has run the study that would let you draw a line. So here is what can honestly be said instead.

## The one conversion that helps

Roughly **14 puffs is about one cigarette**.

Write it with the squiggle. Puff duration moves it, device power moves it, and nicotine
strength moves it a lot. It's a range dressed up as a number, and anyone quoting you a
precise conversion is overstating what's known.

But it's useful, because it turns an abstract count into something you already have
intuition about:

| Puffs a day | Roughly equivalent to |
|---|---|
| 70 | 5 cigarettes |
| 140 | 10 cigarettes, half a pack |
| 280 | 20 cigarettes, a pack |
| 420 | 30 cigarettes |

If 400 puffs sounded unremarkable and "a pack and a half a day" doesn't, that gap is the
whole point of doing the conversion.

## The better question

The puff count misses something. Two people can both vape 200 times a day and have completely
different problems.

> How soon you reach for it after waking predicts dependence better than how much you use
> across the day.
>
> <cite>Time to first use, the Fagerstrom test for nicotine dependence, 1991</cite>

That marker is one of the oldest and best validated in the field, carried over from decades
of smoking research. It works because it measures something a daily total can't: whether
your body is now running the schedule.

Among young vapers, **76% reach for it within 30 minutes of waking**. If that's you, you're
not looking at a willpower problem. You're looking at a dependence pattern, and those
respond to different tactics.

## What "a lot" looks like in the data

The honest population picture is about frequency, not volume. In the 2025 National Youth
Tobacco Survey, **41% of young e-cigarette users were vaping on 20 or more days a month**,
and **27% were vaping daily**.

Youth vaping rates have fallen four years running. Those frequency figures barely moved.
Fewer people are starting; the ones still going are just as stuck.

## The device matters more than you'd think

A single Geek Bar Pulse holds about 800mg of nicotine. Adjusted for what your body actually
absorbs, that's somewhere between 300 and 500 cigarettes in one disposable.

That's not a reason to panic about a puff count. It's a reason to notice that a 5% salt
disposable delivers far more per puff than a 3mg refillable, so two people with identical
counts can be taking in wildly different amounts. If you want the detail on what's inside
these things, we [ranked five categories of vape by what's actually been measured](/blog/most-dangerous-vapes-ranked-2026),
and the disposables tier is the one most people are holding.

## What to do instead

Count for three ordinary days. Not your best days, not the day you decided to do something
about it. Three normal ones.

Almost everyone guesses low. That's not dishonesty, it's just that hand-to-mouth habits run
below conscious attention, which is exactly what makes them hard to shift. The gap between what you think you do and what you really do is usually the most useful thing
you'll learn all week.

Then you've got a real starting number, and a taper has something to work from. That's the
whole reason [Cirrus counts your real puffs rather than asking you to estimate](/) them.

One last thing. Most cravings last **15 to 20 minutes**. Not all day. The window you have to
sit through is much shorter than it feels at the time.
